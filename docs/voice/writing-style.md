# Writing style for drafts in Dom's voice

Hard rules and banned phrases only, copied from Dom's writing-style reference. The critic enforces the hard rules as code; the triage and planner prompts quote this file.

## Hard rules

- British English throughout.
- No em dashes. Use full stops, commas, or restructure.
- No emojis.
- No correlative conjunctions: not only/but also, both/and (as a pair), either/or used as a frame, neither/nor, whether/or, just as/so.
- No soft asks at the close, no calls to action, no "let me know if helpful" or "would love your thoughts".
- The words "quiet" and "quietly" never appear.
- Active voice by default.
- No negative parallelism: "not X, but Y", "it's not this, it's that".
- No rule of three: avoid three parallel items or three structurally identical sentences.
- No importance signalling: "plays a key role", "marks a pivotal moment", "stands as a testament to".
- No vague attribution: "industry observers have noted", "many experts agree".
- No hedge swarms. One position.
- No knowledge-cutoff disclaimers or apologies for incomplete sources.
- No costume metaphors: "X wearing Y", "dressed as", "masquerading as".
- Straight quotes, not curly.
- Title case only in a document title.

## Banned phrases

Cut on sight.

AI residues: in today's fast-paced world, in the realm of, navigate the complexities, unlock the power of, a testament to, stands as, serves as, plays a vital role, underscores the importance, in the ever-evolving landscape, it's worth noting, it's important to note.

Soft connectors: furthermore, moreover, additionally, in addition, that said, having said that, to be sure.

Importance words: crucial, vital, key, pivotal, essential, paramount, significant (when puffing), truly.

Corporate filler: leverage (as a verb), synergy, robust, holistic, ecosystem (when not literal), unlock, enable, empower, drive value, mission-critical, world-class.

Consultant-speak: best-in-class, thought leader, value-add, circle back, double-click, bandwidth (when not literal), low-hanging fruit, north star (when not literal).

British weasel words: arguably, somewhat, rather, quite, fairly, a bit, perhaps.

Forced understatement: quiet, quietly.

Tech press clichés: game-changer, disrupt, revolutionise, paradigm shift, next-gen, cutting-edge, bleeding-edge, deep dive, learnings.

Other: honest, honestly.

## Register for email drafts

Internal: direct, specific, named, numbers where relevant. External: same spine, softer skin; anonymise client detail from other clients; bluntness at the level of the idea, never the person. Most sentences under fifteen words with a longer one when the point needs it. Open on the specific thing, not on a statement that the topic matters. Close on the ask or the fact, never on a sentiment.
